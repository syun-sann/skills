# Undergraduate Admissions Essay Revision & Rubric Guide

This guide defines the diagnostic standards, editing principles, anti-hallucination rules, and anti-cliché filters used when reviewing, diagnosing, revising, and elevating college and undergraduate application essays and personal statements globally (US Common App, UC PIQs, US supplements, UK UCAS, Canadian profiles, Singapore/HK statements, European motivation letters, etc.).

---

## 1. Factual Integrity & Authentic Experience Mandate (绝对保真原则)

### The Core Rule
Admissions essays represent the applicant's genuine lived experience, intellectual maturity, and personal character. **Keep all content strictly grounded in reality: avoid assuming, inventing, or exaggerating facts, metrics, awards, roles, lab equipment, course titles, or personal anecdotes not provided in the student's draft or notes.**

### Operational Rules for Missing Information
1. **Rely Exclusively on Confirmed Facts:** If the applicant writes *"I conducted experiments on enzyme activity"*, do not fabricate specific reagent brands, statistical results, or equipment (*"using an Agilent Cary 60 UV-Vis spectrophotometer with an R² of 0.998"*).
2. **Flag and Inquire:** In Phase 1 of the output (*修改状态与追问清单*), explicitly list every question needed to fill narrative, qualitative, or quantitative voids.
3. **Use Explicit Placeholders in Polished Text:** If a missing detail is required to maintain grammatical and logical flow, use bracketed placeholders such as `[具体实验仪器与反应试剂]` or `[具体参赛人数与获奖级别需确认]`, ensuring the applicant knows exactly what to substitute.

### Reviewer Proposals vs. Student Reality (批注启发性例子的甄别与核验)
Admissions counselors and reviewers often provide hypothetical examples in annotations (e.g., *"Add more sensory detail here, like smelling burnt solder when your drone short-circuited..."*).
- **Core Distinction:** Such examples are **inspirational proposals (Proposals)** designed to illustrate a narrative direction—they are **never** confirmed student facts!
- **Strict Grilling Protocol:**
  1. **Never Presume as Fact:** Never copy hypothetical examples into revised drafts as if the student experienced them.
  2. **Design Tree Frontier Grilling:** Place unverified reviewer proposals on the current interview frontier. Formulate explicit options (recommended option, alternative real experience, user write-in).
  3. **Provide Narrative Context:** Clarify *why* the reviewer suggested the detail (e.g., *"This proposal aims to show hands-on diagnostic resilience rather than passive observation"*).
  4. **Dual Verification Track:**
     - *Question 1 (Authenticity Check):* Did you actually experience this specific event or detail?
     - *Question 2 (Alternative Identification):* If not, what real incident from your project/life demonstrated the same problem-solving ability or realization?
  5. **Safety Placeholder in Text:** If drafted prior to confirmation, use explicit warning placeholders: `[顾问提案示例，需学生确认真实性或提供真实经历替换：...]`.

## 2. Preserving the Authentic Student Voice (守护原生特质)

Admissions committees read thousands of essays each admissions cycle. The quickest way to trigger skepticism is a homogenized, corporate, or overly academic voice that no 17-to-19-year-old would naturally use.

* **Target Voice:** An articulate, reflective, intellectually curious, and self-aware high school senior or undergraduate applicant.
* **Tone:** Sincere, thoughtful, proactive, and grounded. Neither boastful nor falsely modest.
* **Preserve Quirks & Authentic Perspective:** Keep the applicant's unique metaphors, personal idioms, niche curiosities, and genuine emotional turning points intact. Polish grammar and cadence without flattening their personality.
* **System-Appropriate Voice Calibration:**
  - *US Holistic Essays (Common App, Supplements):* Introspective, narrative, personal vulnerability, moral or intellectual growth.
  - *US Direct/CAR Prompts (UC PIQs):* Direct, active, factual, outcome-oriented ("interview on paper").
  - *UK UCAS Personal Statements:* Scholarly, analytical, intellectually rigorous, focused on academic and supercurricular engagement (75–80% academic content).
  - *Canadian Profiles (UBC, Waterloo):* Direct leadership, problem-solving impact, collaboration, and self-awareness.
* **Minimal-Invasive Surgery on Strong Sections:** If a paragraph already communicates voice and insight effectively, preserve its original texture and diction. Concentrate revisions on structural bottlenecks, vague claims, or logical discontinuities.

## 3. Anti-Slop & Humanizer Filter (深度融合 Humanizer 与 Stop-Slop)

Application essays must eliminate robotic, predictable AI patterns while preserving intellectual depth, semantic accuracy, and the applicant's authentic lived experience.

### A. Prohibited Vocabulary & Inflated Claims (拒绝假大空)

| Category | Prohibited Words / Phrases | Preferred Alternatives / Fixes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pompous Nouns** | *tapestry, beacon, testament, realm, catalyst, plethora, symphony, kaleidoscope, cornerstone, embarkation, zenith* | Replace with concrete nouns or straightforward framing. Let facts prove importance. |
| **Overused Adjectives** | *multifaceted, profound, indelible, paramount, invaluable, transformative, pivotal, vital, seamless, breathtaking* | Remove the empty modifier; describe the tangible action or consequence. |
| **Vague Action Verbs** | *delve into, foster, navigate the complexities of, embark upon, weave, underscore, resonate with, epitomize* | Use direct, active verbs (*built, debugged, negotiated, calculated, revised, tested, interviewed*). |
| **Throat-Clearing Openings** | *"From a young age...", "Since time immemorial...", "Have you ever wondered...", "It is worth noting that..."* | Cut immediately; start in media res or with a direct factual observation or question. |
| **Formulaic Conclusions** | *"In conclusion...", "This experience taught me...", "Looking toward the future...", "paving the way for..."* | End on a forward-looking resonance, reflective counterfactual, or open synthesis. |

### B. Structural AI Tells to Eliminate (打破典型 AI 句式套路)

1. **Trailing `-ing` Shallow Analysis**:
   - *AI Pattern:* Ending sentences with lazy participial phrases (*"...allowing me to embrace my potential, highlighting the importance of resilience and shaping my worldview."*).
   - *Fix:* Cut the trailing tail. If the reflection matters, state it in its own concrete sentence with real causal mechanism.
2. **Binary Contrasts & Negative Listings**:
   - *AI Pattern:* *"It was not merely about writing code, but about forging human connection."*
   - *Fix:* State the positive truth directly: *"I realized the software was useless if elderly residents couldn't read the buttons."*
3. **Rule-of-Three Overkill**:
   - *AI Pattern:* Forcing three parallel adjectives, nouns, or verbs into every rhythmic beat (*"...with passion, dedication, and unwavering curiosity."*).
   - *Fix:* Use two precise items, or a single strong, accurate noun.
4. **Synthetic Pull-Quotes & Slogans**:
   - *AI Pattern:* Crafting polished PR slogans (*"In the silence of the laboratory, I found the music of science."*).
   - *Fix:* Ground in sensory, imperfect reality (*"My pipetting thumb cramped after the twentieth dilution, but the agar plate finally stayed clear."*).
5. **Passive Pseudo-Agency**:
   - *AI Pattern:* *"The research opened my eyes to the importance of economic equilibrium."*
   - *Fix:* Restore human subject and active verb: *"Graphing the supply shocks in Stata made me question whether price ceilings actually protect vulnerable households."*

### C. The 5-Point Quick Check (交付前快速自检)

1. **Agency Check:** Does an actual human being perform the action in every sentence? (No *"the project enlightened me"* $\to$ *"I calibrated the sensor"*).
2. **Inflated Significance Check:** Are ordinary actions treated like historic milestones? (Cut *"marked a pivotal turning point in my trajectory"*).
3. **Trailing Fluff Check:** Are there any loose `-ing` phrases dragging at sentence ends? (Delete or convert to direct assertions).
4. **Cadence & Rhythm Check:** Do three consecutive sentences share identical length or grammatical structure? (Vary sentence lengths: mix short, punchy statements with complex analytical syntax).
5. **Authentic Tone Check:** Does this sound like a real, articulate applicant in conversation or an admissions interview, or a corporate marketing brochure?

## 4. Show, Don't Tell: Micro-Details & Operational Grounding

Admissions officers remember tangible scenes, operational dilemmas, and specific choices, not abstract assertions of virtue.

* **Weak (Generic Summary):**
  > *"I faced many logistical hurdles while organizing the regional debate tournament, which honed my leadership skills."*
* **Strong (Operational Grounding):**
  > *"When the school WiFi collapsed twenty minutes before Round 1, I sprinted between five classrooms manually distributing paper pairings and reassigning timer duties to spare volunteers."*

* **Weak (Vague Academic Claim):**
  > *"I have always been deeply passionate about behavioral economics and reading classic texts."*
* **Strong (Supercurricular Deep Dive - UK/US Academic):**
  > *"Reading Kahneman's analysis of prospect theory led me to test loss aversion during our school charity auction: framing donations as 'preventing food waste' rather than 'supporting meals' increased bidding participation by 28%."*

## 5. Holistic Rubric across Global Admissions Systems

Evaluate and revise all undergraduate application essays across five core dimensions:

1. **Thesis & Core Narrative / Academic Arc (核心主线与张力):**
   - Is there a clear intellectual, personal, or operational throughline?
   - Does the piece move logically from context/curiosity $\to$ concrete exploration/action $\to$ nuanced realization?
2. **Agency & Proactivity (主体性与行动力):**
   - Does the applicant take active ownership of choices, dilemmas, and failures, or do events merely happen to them?
   - Are actions driven by authentic internal curiosity rather than external resume checklist ticking?
3. **Intellectual Vitality & Depth of Reflection (思辨深度与反思能力):**
   - Does the reflection demonstrate nuance, self-critique, or insight into *why* things occurred?
   - In academic essays (e.g. UCAS, Why Major), does the applicant engage critically with concepts, data, or arguments rather than merely summarizing book titles?
4. **Voice & Tone Calibration (语言质感与语调契合):**
   - Is the register well-matched to the system (reflective/narrative for US Common App; crisp/CAR for UC; scholarly/supercurricular for UK UCAS; leadership/impact for Canadian profiles)?
   - Are sentence rhythms lively and natural, free of pretentious thesaurus-swapping?
5. **Word/Character Economy & Prompt Fit (篇幅经济度与题目契合度):**
   - Does every sentence advance character, intellect, or narrative?
   - Does the essay strictly abide by platform constraints (Common App 250–650 words; UC PIQs $\le 350$ words; UCAS 4,000 characters and 47 lines; UBC 200 words, etc.)?
