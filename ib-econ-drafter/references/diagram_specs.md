# IB Economics Diagram Specifications & Blueprint Library

To score full marks on AO4 and diagram analysis, examiners expect **accurate labels, explicit shift arrows, clearly marked equilibrium coordinates, and precise welfare areas**. 

Every drafted diagram must supply three elements:
1. **Diagram Blueprint Table** (Axes, curves, coordinates, shift vectors, shaded areas).
2. **Step-by-Step Drawing Instructions** (What the student draws first, second, and marks).
3. **In-Text Diagram Walkthrough** (Explicitly explaining the movement from initial equilibrium to final equilibrium using diagram labels).

## 1. Microeconomics Diagrams

### 1.1 Specific Indirect Tax
- **Y-Axis**: Price of [Good X] ($ / unit)
- **X-Axis**: Quantity of [Good X] (units per time period)
- **Curves**:
  - Demand: $D = MPB = MSB$ (downward sloping)
  - Supply: $S_1 = MPC$ (upward sloping)
  - Post-tax Supply: $S_2 = S_1 + \text{tax}$ (parallel upward shift by vertical distance equal to specific tax $t$)
- **Equilibria**:
  - Initial Equilibrium: $E_1$ at $(Q_1, P_1)$ where $S_1 = D$
  - New Market Price: $P_c$ (price paid by consumers) where $S_2$ intersects $D$ at quantity $Q_2$
  - Producer Received Price: $P_p = P_c - t$ on curve $S_1$ at quantity $Q_2$
- **Key Areas & Welfare**:
  - Consumer Tax Incidence: Rectangle $(P_1 \text{ to } P_c) \times Q_2$
  - Producer Tax Incidence: Rectangle $(P_p \text{ to } P_1) \times Q_2$
  - Total Government Tax Revenue: Rectangle $(P_p \text{ to } P_c) \times Q_2$
  - Welfare Loss / Deadweight Loss (DWL): Triangle between $Q_2$ and $Q_1$, bounded by $D$ and $S_1$.

### 1.2 Production Subsidy
- **Y-Axis**: Price of [Good X] ($ / unit)
- **X-Axis**: Quantity of [Good X] (units per time period)
- **Curves**:
  - Demand: $D = MPB$
  - Supply: $S_1 = MPC$
  - Post-subsidy Supply: $S_2 = S_1 - \text{subsidy}$ (shifts vertically downward by unit subsidy $s$)
- **Equilibria**:
  - Initial Equilibrium: $E_1$ at $(Q_1, P_1)$
  - New Market Equilibrium: $E_2$ at $(Q_2, P_c)$ where $S_2$ intersects $D$ ($P_c < P_1$)
  - Producer Price Received: $P_p = P_c + s$ on curve $S_1$ at quantity $Q_2$ ($P_p > P_1$)
- **Key Areas & Welfare**:
  - Consumer Gain in Surplus: Area between $P_1$ and $P_c$ up to $Q_2$
  - Producer Gain in Surplus: Area between $P_p$ and $P_1$ up to $Q_2$
  - Total Government Expenditure: Rectangle $(P_c \text{ to } P_p) \times Q_2$
  - Deadweight Loss (DWL): Triangle bounded by $S_1$, $D$, and between $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ (costs exceed benefits).

### 1.3 Price Ceiling (Maximum Price $P_{\max}$)
- **Y-Axis**: Price of [Essential Good, e.g. Bread/Rental Housing] ($)
- **X-Axis**: Quantity of [Good X] (units)
- **Curves**: Downward sloping $D$, upward sloping $S$
- **Equilibria & Disruption**:
  - Free market equilibrium: $(Q_e, P_e)$
  - Price Ceiling line: Horizontal dashed line set strictly below equilibrium at $P_{\max}$
  - Quantity Supplied: $Q_s$ (intersection of $P_{\max}$ with $S$)
  - Quantity Demanded: $Q_d$ (intersection of $P_{\max}$ with $D$)
  - Excess Demand / Shortage: Distance $(Q_d - Q_s)$
- **Key Areas & Welfare**:
  - DWL: Triangle between $Q_s$ and $Q_e$ under $D$ down to $S$.

### 1.4 Price Floor (Minimum Price $P_{\min}$)
- **Y-Axis**: Price of [Agricultural Product / Minimum Wage] ($)
- **X-Axis**: Quantity of [Agricultural Product / Labor Hours] (units / workers)
- **Equilibria & Disruption**:
  - Free market equilibrium: $(Q_e, P_e)$
  - Price Floor line: Horizontal dashed line set strictly above equilibrium at $P_{\min}$
  - Quantity Demanded: $Q_d$
  - Quantity Supplied: $Q_s$
  - Excess Supply / Surplus (or Unemployment in labor): Distance $(Q_s - Q_d)$
- **Government Intervention (if buying surplus)**:
  - Government Expenditure: $P_{\min} \times (Q_s - Q_d)$.

### 1.5 Negative Externality of Production (e.g. Carbon/Pollution)
- **Y-Axis**: Costs / Benefits / Price ($)
- **X-Axis**: Quantity of Output ($Q$)
- **Curves**:
  - Marginal Private Benefit: $MPB = MSB$ (assuming no consumption externality)
  - Marginal Private Cost: $MPC$
  - Marginal Social Cost: $MSC = MPC + MEC$ (shifted above and to the left of $MPC$)
- **Equilibria**:
  - Free market output: $Q_m$ at intersection of $MPC$ and $MPB$ (Price $P_m$)
  - Socially optimal output: $Q^*$ at intersection of $MSC$ and $MSB$ (Price $P^*$)
  - Overproduction / Overallocation of resources: $Q_m > Q^*$
- **Welfare Loss**:
  - Deadweight Loss Triangle: Points pointing towards social optimum $Q^*$, bounded by $MSC$ and $MPB$ between $Q^*$ and $Q_m$.
- **Corrective Policy (Pigouvian Tax)**:
  - Tax equal to marginal external cost ($MEC$) shifts $MPC$ upward to align with $MSC$, moving market output to $Q^*$.

### 1.6 Positive Externality of Consumption (e.g. Healthcare/Education)
- **Y-Axis**: Costs / Benefits / Price ($)
- **X-Axis**: Quantity of Output ($Q$)
- **Curves**:
  - Marginal Private Cost: $MPC = MSC$ (assuming no production externality)
  - Marginal Private Benefit: $MPB$
  - Marginal Social Benefit: $MSB = MPB + MEB$ (shifted above and to the right of $MPB$)
- **Equilibria**:
  - Free market output: $Q_m$ where $MPB = MPC$
  - Social optimum: $Q^*$ where $MSB = MSC$ ($Q^* > Q_m$)
  - Underallocation / Underconsumption: Distance $(Q^* - Q_m)$
- **Potential Welfare Gain**: Triangle pointing at market output $Q_m$, bounded by $MSB$ and $MPC$ between $Q_m$ and $Q^*$.

## 2. Macroeconomics Diagrams

### 2.1 Neoclassical / Monetarist AD/AS Model
- **Y-Axis**: Average Price Level ($APL$ or $CPI$)
- **X-Axis**: Real GDP ($Y$ or Real Output)
- **Curves**:
  - Aggregate Demand: $AD_1$ (downward sloping)
  - Short-Run Aggregate Supply: $SRAS_1$ (upward sloping)
  - Long-Run Aggregate Supply: $LRAS$ (vertical line at potential output $Y_p$)
- **Scenarios**:
  - **Deflationary / Recessionary Gap**: Initial equilibrium $E_1(Y_1, APL_1)$ where $AD_1$ intersects $SRAS_1$ to the left of $Y_p$ ($Y_1 < Y_p$).
    - *Expansionary policy shift*: $AD_1$ shifts right to $AD_2$, restoring output to $Y_p$ at higher price level $APL_2$.
    - *Market self-correction (long run)*: High unemployment puts downward pressure on wages, shifting $SRAS_1$ right to $SRAS_2$, restoring $Y_p$ at lower price level $APL_3$.
  - **Inflationary Gap**: Initial equilibrium to the right of $Y_p$ ($Y_1 > Y_p$). $AD$ shifts left via contractionary policy, or $SRAS$ shifts left due to rising factor costs.

### 2.2 Keynesian AD/AS Model
- **Y-Axis**: Average Price Level ($APL$)
- **X-Axis**: Real GDP / Real Output ($Y$)
- **Keynesian AS Curve**: Reverse L-shaped curve with three phases:
  1. *Horizontal segment*: Severe spare capacity / recession (output expands with zero inflation pressure).
  2. *Upward sloping curved segment*: Approaching full employment, bottlenecks arise (output and price level rise together).
  3. *Vertical segment*: Full employment $Y_{fe}$ (maximum capacity, further $AD$ increases cause purely demand-pull inflation).
- **Equilibrium**: Where $AD$ intersects Keynesian $AS$. If intersecting on the horizontal section, equilibrium exists with persistent deflationary gap and no automatic self-correction.

## 3. Global Economy Diagrams

### 3.1 Tariff on Imported Goods
- **Y-Axis**: Price of [Good, e.g. Steel] ($)
- **X-Axis**: Quantity of [Good] (units)
- **Curves**:
  - Domestic Demand: $D_d$
  - Domestic Supply: $S_d$
  - World Supply (Free Trade): Horizontal perfectly elastic line $S_w$ at world price $P_w$
  - World Supply + Tariff: Horizontal line $S_w + t$ at price $P_{w+t}$
- **Equilibria**:
  - Free Trade: Domestic consumers buy $Q_4$ at $P_w$; domestic producers produce $Q_1$; imports = $Q_4 - Q_1$.
  - Post-Tariff: Price rises to $P_{w+t}$. Domestic production expands from $Q_1 \to Q_2$; domestic consumption contracts from $Q_4 \to Q_3$; imports contract to $Q_3 - Q_2$.
- **Welfare & Revenue**:
  - Consumer Surplus Loss: Trapezoid $(P_w \text{ to } P_{w+t}) \times Q_4$
  - Domestic Producer Gain: Trapezoid $(P_w \text{ to } P_{w+t}) \times Q_1$
  - Government Tariff Revenue: Rectangle $(P_{w+t} - P_w) \times (Q_3 - Q_2)$
  - Deadweight Loss (Production Inefficiency $DWL_1$): Triangle between $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ under $S_d$.
  - Deadweight Loss (Consumption Inefficiency $DWL_2$): Triangle between $Q_3$ and $Q_4$ under $D_d$.

### 3.2 Floating Exchange Rate (Currency Market)
- **Y-Axis**: Exchange Rate (Price of Currency A in terms of Currency B, e.g. USD/EUR)
- **X-Axis**: Quantity of Currency A traded
- **Curves**:
  - Demand for Currency A: $D_1$ (downward sloping, driven by foreign demand for exports, inward FDI, tourism, interest rate differentials)
  - Supply of Currency A: $S_1$ (upward sloping, driven by domestic demand for imports, outward investment)
- **Shifts & Changes**:
  - Appreciation: $D_1$ shifts right to $D_2$ (or $S_1$ shifts left to $S_2$), raising exchange rate from $ER_1 \to ER_2$.
  - Depreciation: $D_1$ shifts left to $D_2$ (or $S_1$ shifts right to $S_2$), lowering exchange rate from $ER_1 \to ER_2$.
