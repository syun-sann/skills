# NUS Application Essay Revision Guide

Expert guide for reviewing, tightening, and polishing application essays and mandatory short responses for the **National University of Singapore (NUS)**.

NUS explicitly instructs applicants:
> *"This is a self-evaluation of your interests and suitability for the programme, so please answer simply and honestly in Standard English, and in your own words. Do not use any external aid, such as Artificial Intelligence or Ghostwriting; doing so may have a negative impact on your application. Each question has a character reply limit, so avoid verbose answers and write straight to the point."*

---

## 1. Official NUS Prompts & Character Reply Limits

| Prompt | Official NUS Question | Strict Character Limit (with spaces) | Key Strategic Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Question 1** | *Tell us something you have done outside your school curriculum to prepare yourself for your chosen degree programme(s).* | **Max 600 characters** (~80–95 words) | Direct, concrete academic/technical self-preparation, projects, tools, and technical insight gained. |
| **Question 2** | *Describe an instance when you did not succeed in accomplishing something on your first attempt but succeeded on subsequent attempts. How and what did you learn from your initial failure, and what changes did you make to your approach to eventually succeed?* | **Max 600 characters** (~80–95 words) | Clear failure diagnosis, deliberate methodological/strategic pivot, second-attempt success, and enduring lesson. |
| **Question 3** | *Share something that is meaningful to you and explain how it has impacted you in a concrete way.* | **Max 600 characters** (~80–95 words) | Genuinely personal significance + tangible, real-world impact on actions, perspective, and values. |
| **Question 4** | *What is your proudest achievement, and how did you accomplish it with the help or inspiration from others? Please also explain how it exemplifies some of the five NUS values of Innovation, Resilience, Excellence, Respect and Integrity.* | **Max 1,100 characters** (~150–175 words) | High-impact achievement + collaborative mentorship + explicit integration of **NUS Values (Innovation, Resilience, Excellence, Respect, Integrity)**. |

## 2. Core Revision Principles

### A. Standard British English (UK Academic Conventions)
Singapore institutions strictly adhere to **Standard British English**.
- **Spelling**: Use British conventions (e.g., *realise*, *analyse*, *prioritise*, *optimise*, *programme*, *centre*, *rigour*, *enrolment*, *travelled*, *defence*, *licence* [noun] vs *license* [verb]).
- **Terminology**: Use British/Commonwealth academic phrasing (e.g., *at university* not *in college*; *course/module/programme* not *class*; *read Computer Science / study Law*; *sit an examination* not *take a test*; *secondary school / junior college*; *maths* not *math*).
- **Punctuation**: Punctuation placed outside quotation marks unless part of the original quote.
- *See [references/british_english_guide.md](references/british_english_guide.md) for a comprehensive reference.*

### B. Simplicity, Honesty & High Information Density
- **Simplicity & Directness**: Answer the question directly in the very first sentence. Eliminate throat-clearing openings (*"Ever since I was a child..."*, *"In order to prepare myself..."*).
- **Zero Fluff**: Every sentence must advance the narrative, share concrete evidence/metrics, or demonstrate problem-solving agency.
- **Authenticity over Grandeur**: Avoid melodrama, pseudo-intellectual buzzwords (*testament, beacon, tapestry, multifaceted*), or inflated claims. Ground all reflections in real actions and data.

### C. Explicit NUS Values Integration (Specifically for Question 4)
When revising Question 4, ensure the essay organically weaves in relevant **NUS Core Values**:
1. **Innovation**: Creative problem-solving, novel methods.
2. **Resilience**: Bouncing back from setbacks, perseverance.
3. **Excellence**: Pursuit of high standards, rigor.
4. **Respect**: Cultural humility, valuing diverse viewpoints.
5. **Integrity**: Honesty, ethical execution, transparency.

### D. Minimal-Invasive Editing (Preserving the Applicant's Voice)
- **Do not rewrite from scratch**: Retain the applicant's original stories, ideas, and authentic perspective.
- **Targeted elevation**: Fix grammar/spelling, tighten sentence structures, trim redundant phrasing, and heighten impact while keeping the applicant's unique voice recognizable.

## 3. Step-by-Step Revision Workflow

1. **Step 1: Identify Prompt & Check Limits**:
   - Determine which NUS question (Q1–Q4 or custom) is being addressed.
   - Set the strict character ceiling (**600 characters** for Q1–Q3; **1,100 characters** for Q4; or user-specified limit).
   - Calculate baseline character counts (with/without spaces) and word count.

2. **Step 2: Strategic Diagnostics & Tightening**:
   - Strip filler phrases (*"in order to", "the reason why is because", "I was able to"*).
   - Convert American spelling/terms to Standard British English.
   - Ensure the specific requirements of the prompt (e.g., failure analysis in Q2, NUS values in Q4) are clearly met.
   - Refine syntax to maximize information density.

3. **Step 3: Verification & Output**:
   - Verify that the revised text is strictly **at or below the character limit (including spaces)**.
   - Output the revised text, the metrics comparison table, and concise bullet points explaining refinements.
