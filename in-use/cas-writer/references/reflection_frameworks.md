# CAS Reflection Frameworks & Methodologies

Effective reflection in IB CAS is not a casual diary entry or a dry summary of events. It is a structured cognitive process that transforms raw experience into meaningful personal, ethical, and intellectual growth.

This guide provides the core frameworks recognized by IB educators worldwide.

---

## 1. The 5 IB CAS Stages Framework

For any substantial CAS experience (and mandatory for the collaborative CAS Project), students navigate five distinct stages:

```
[ Investigation ] ➔ [ Preparation ] ➔ [ Action ] ➔ [ Reflection ] ➔ [ Demonstration ]
        └─── Continuous Reflective Loop Throughout All Stages ───┘
```

| Stage | Focus & Questions to Answer | Typical Artifacts & Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Investigation** | • What is the authentic need, creative curiosity, or physical goal?<br>• What skills, resources, and baseline knowledge do I currently possess?<br>• What are the potential ethical and logistical challenges? | Survey results, interview notes, mood boards, fitness baseline logs |
| **2. Preparation** | • Who does what, by when, and with what resources?<br>• How do we establish risk mitigation and safety protocols?<br>• What roles are assigned based on team members' individual strengths? | Gantt charts, meeting minutes, permission slips, budget sheets, training plans |
| **3. Action** | • How was the plan executed in reality?<br>• What spontaneous obstacles emerged, and how were they navigated?<br>• How did collaborative decision-making occur under pressure? | Action photos, workshop video clips, match logs, code commits, draft manuscripts |
| **4. Reflection** | • What were the key emotional and cognitive turning points?<br>• What specific IB Learning Outcomes were developed?<br>• How have my assumptions or worldviews evolved? | Journal entries, audio reflections, annotated photo essays, peer feedback forms |
| **5. Demonstration** | • How is the impact and learning communicated to the wider community?<br>• What tangible final product or portfolio evidence validates the journey? | Final exhibition, assembly presentation, website launch, published zine, certificate |

## 2. Gibbs' Reflective Cycle (6-Step Deep Model)

Gibbs' Cycle (1988) is the gold standard for structuring deep CAS reflections, particularly for high-intensity or challenging milestones.

```
       1. Description (What happened?)
                  │
       2. Feelings (What were you thinking/feeling?)
                  │
       3. Evaluation (What was good and bad?)
                  │
       4. Analysis (What sense can you make of it?)
                  │
       5. Conclusion (What else could you have done?)
                  │
       6. Action Plan (If it happened again, what next?)
```

### Deconstructing the 6 Steps for CAS:

1. **Description (15% of reflection)**:
   - Provide concrete, objective context: Who, where, when, and specific action.
   - *Example*: *"During our third weekly coding workshop for primary school students, only 4 of the 12 attendees could log into the Scratch environment due to firewall restrictions on the lab computers."*

2. **Feelings & Thoughts (15% of reflection)**:
   - Capture real-time emotional states without censorship: embarrassment, anxiety, impatience, or unexpected relief.
   - *Example*: *"I felt an immediate surge of panic as lesson time ticked away, followed by self-doubt about whether I had adequately prepared the lab environment."*

3. **Evaluation (20% of reflection)**:
   - Weigh what worked versus what fell short with honest objectivity.
   - *Example*: *"While our slide deck was well-designed, our failure to verify IT permissions before class paralyzed our planned schedule. However, pivoting to unplugged logic games kept the students engaged while we resolved the logins."*

4. **Analysis (25% of reflection)**:
   - Connect the occurrence to underlying causes, group dynamics, or IB Learning Outcomes.
   - *Example*: *"This incident highlighted the difference between theoretical planning and operational execution (LO 3). I realized that assuming technical infrastructure works without prior on-site testing is a fatal flaw in project management."*

5. **Conclusion (15% of reflection)**:
   - Extract the personal revelation and identify alternative choices that could have been made.
   - *Example*: *"I learned that staying calm under pressure reassures younger learners, but proactive coordination with school IT staff is non-negotiable for future sessions."*

6. **Action Plan (10% of reflection)**:
   - Formulate concrete, testable adjustments for the subsequent session.
   - *Example*: *"For next Thursday's session, I will arrive 30 minutes early to test all 15 student terminals and prepare a laminated 'unplugged backup activity pack' in my binder."*

## 3. Rolfe et al.’s "What? So What? Now What?" Framework

Ideal for concise, high-impact reflections (150–250 words) suitable for regular weekly logs on ManageBac:

### Stage 1: What? (Descriptive Fact)
* What happened? What did I do? What did others do? What was the outcome?
* *Key phrase*: "During this week's rehearsal..." / "While conducting the neighborhood recycling audit..."

### Stage 2: So What? (Critical Meaning & Impact)
* Why does this matter? What did I feel? What did I discover about my skills or biases? Which Learning Outcome was exercised?
* *Key phrase*: "This mattered because..." / "It made me realize that my assumption about X was flawed because..."

### Stage 3: Now What? (Forward-Looking Application)
* What are the broader implications? How will this reshape my future approach, habits, or ethics?
* *Key phrase*: "Going forward, I will..." / "Next week, I plan to test this by..."

## 4. Kolb's Experiential Learning Cycle

Used especially for skill acquisition in **Activity** (athletics, physical disciplines) and **Creativity** (visual art, instrument performance, coding):

```
       [ Concrete Experience ] (Doing the drill / performance)
                 │
                 ▼
     [ Reflective Observation ] (Reviewing video / noticing muscle fatigue)
                 │
                 ▼
   [ Abstract Conceptualization ] (Understanding the biomechanical or theoretical principle)
                 │
                 ▼
     [ Active Experimentation ] (Applying modified technique in next session)
```

## 5. Summary: Choosing the Right Framework

| Use Case | Recommended Framework | Word Count Target |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Weekly / Routine Activity Log** | Rolfe's *What? So What? Now What?* | 120 – 200 words |
| **Significant Milestone / Setback / Breakthrough** | Gibbs' *6-Step Reflective Cycle* | 250 – 400 words |
| **Formal CAS Project Documentation** | 5 CAS Stages + Gibbs Combination | 350 – 500 words per stage |
| **Technical Skill / Athletic Progression** | Kolb's *Experiential Cycle* | 150 – 250 words |
