## 2. Essay & Prompt Classification Taxonomy

Identify the jurisdiction and genre using this two-tier matrix:

### Tier 1: Jurisdiction & Application System
* **US National / Multi-School:** Common Application (Main PS), Coalition Application / Scoir, University of California (UC PIQ 8-prompt system).
* **US University-Specific Supplements:** Ivy Plus, T30 private/public universities, liberal arts colleges.
* **UK Multi-School (UCAS):** Unified 5-choice application for British universities (Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial, LSE, UCL, etc.), classic 4,000-char/47-line statement or 3-question reform.
* **Canada Broad-Based:** UBC Personal Profile, University of Waterloo AIF (Engineering/Math/AFM), UofT Supplementary Profiles (Rotman, Engineering).
* **Singapore & Hong Kong:** NUS, NTU, HKU, HKUST, CUHK application essays and achievement statements.
* **Europe & International Motivation Letters:** Netherlands Numerus Fixus (TU Delft, UvA, Erasmus), Sciences Po bachelor essays, Swedish/German English-taught bachelors.
* **General / Open Statement of Purpose:** Independent undergraduate applications, scholarship essays, open-ended personal statements.

### Tier 2: Functional Essay Genre
1. **Narrative & Introspective Growth:** (e.g. Common App PS, Coalition). Focus: vulnerability, reflection, personality, self-awareness, transformative realization.
2. **Behavioral Competency / CAR ("Interview on Paper"):** (e.g. UC PIQs, UBC Personal Profile). Focus: Context $\to$ Action $\to$ Result/Impact; active first-person ownership; no dramatic fiction.
3. **Supercurricular & Academic Rigor:** (e.g. UK UCAS, Oxbridge, Why Major). Focus: 75–80% subject-specific engagement (books, papers, experiments, theory critique); academic curiosity; intellectual stamina.
4. **Institutional Alignment ("Why Us"):** (e.g. US college supplements, European motivation letters). Focus: precise match between student track record/goals and target institution's unique curriculum, faculty labs, or academic culture.
5. **Community, Diversity & Micro-Identity:** Focus: specific micro-community contributions, cross-cultural empathy, perspective-sharing.
6. **Activity / Project Deep Dive:** (e.g. Waterloo AIF, MIT/Stanford activity elaboration). Focus: technical hurdles, operational logistics, measurable impact.
7. **Intellectual Playfulness & Short Quirky Prompts:** (e.g. UChicago, Stanford, Yale, Columbia). Focus: distinct intellectual personality, concise wit, original perspective.

## 3. Targeted Official Search Protocol

When any key parameter (prompt wording, word/character/line limits, college-specific prompt changes, evaluation rubric) is unstated, partial, or ambiguous, conduct precision web searches against **primary institutional sources**.

### Search Query Strategies

| Objective | Target Sources | Recommended Search Query Template |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Verify Specific University Supplemental Prompts** | Official undergraduate admissions domain (`.edu`, `.ca`) | `site:[university.edu] "first-year" OR "freshman" "supplemental essay" OR "writing supplement" 2025 OR 2026` |
| **Verify Exact Word/Character Limits** | Official admissions portal | `site:[admissions.domain] "[prompt keywords]" "word limit" OR "words" OR "characters"` |
| **Verify UK Course Expectations (UCAS / Oxbridge)** | Official departmental page (`.ac.uk`) | `site:[ox.ac.uk / cam.ac.uk / lse.ac.uk] "[subject name]" "personal statement" OR "selection criteria"` |
| **Verify Canadian Profile Prompts (UBC / Waterloo)** | Official university applicant portal | `site:you.ubc.ca "personal profile" questions` or `site:uwaterloo.ca "admission information form" engineering questions` |
| **Verify Asian University Essay Requirements (NUS/HKU)** | Official admissions office (`.edu.sg`, `.hku.hk`) | `site:nus.edu.sg/oam "personal statement" OR "achievements" words` |

### Primary vs. Secondary Source Hierarchy
1. **Tier 1 (Authoritative):** Official university admissions portals (`admission.stanford.edu`, `you.ubc.ca`, `ucas.com`, `commonapp.org`, `admission.universityofcalifornia.edu`).
2. **Tier 2 (Official Counselor Guides):** Official university admissions blogs (e.g., MIT Admissions blog, Georgia Tech Admissions blog) and official counselor handbooks.
3. **Tier 3 (Third-Party / Commercial):** Prepscholar, CollegeVine, Reddit, etc. **Use third-party sites ONLY to confirm recent prompt wording changes, then verify against official portals.** Never rely on commercial sites for official word limits without cross-validation.

## 4. Extracting the Holistic Admissions Lens

When official data is gathered, synthesize it into four core operational dimensions:

1. **Platform Constraints & Hard Ceilings:**
   - Word count vs. Character count (with or without spaces).
   - Line limits (e.g. UCAS 47 lines, where each paragraph break consumes 1 line).
   - Plain text restrictions (Common App, UCAS, and UC strip bold, italics, and special markdown formatting).
2. **Audience & Reader Persona:**
   - *Generalist admissions officers / Regional directors* (US Common App, holistic evaluation, looking for character, community fit, and emotional maturity).
   - *Faculty subject academics / Departmental tutors* (UK UCAS, European universities, looking for deep intellectual capability, analytical skills, and readiness for rigorous scholarship).
   - *Score-sheet evaluators / Holistic scorers* (UC PIQs, UBC, Waterloo, grading against explicit rubrics for leadership, impact, and initiative).
3. **Content Weighting:**
   - What proportion belongs to academic exploration vs. personal narrative vs. extracurricular activities?
4. **Institutional Culture & Core Values:**
   - What values does the university publicly emphasize (e.g., interdisciplinarity, undergraduate research, social responsibility, entrepreneurial grit)?

## 6. Handling Ambiguities & Edge Cases

* **Case 1: User provides an essay draft with NO prompt or university name:**
  - Diagnose the text: identify topic, discipline, writing style, and word count.
  - Search distinctive prompt phrases or themes to hypothesize likely targets.
  - On the Round 1 Frontier, place **Question 1** as the Target Verification Question:
    * *Option A (Recommended):* [Most probable system/school based on diagnostic, with verified limits].
    * *Option B:* [Alternative likely system].
    * *Option C (Write-in):* [User inputs specific school/prompt/word limit].
* **Case 2: The university offers multiple prompt options (e.g. Common App 1–7, UC 4 of 8):**
  - Verify all official options.
  - Analyze which prompt best aligns with the student's core experiences and draft trajectory.
  - State the recommendation clearly on the Frontier.
* **Case 3: Unofficial or outdated prompt provided by student:**
  - If web research reveals the prompt changed for the current cycle, flag the official wording immediately on the Frontier, explain the discrepancy, and confirm the target cycle.
