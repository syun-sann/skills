# Official NUS Admissions Essay Prompts & Evaluation Criteria

This reference outlines the exact structure, character reply limits, and strategic evaluation criteria for the **National University of Singapore (NUS) Admissions Short Response Questions**.

---

## 1. Mandatory Short Questions & Character Limits

As part of the official NUS admissions process, applicants complete mandatory short response questions. NUS explicitly states:
> *"This is a self-evaluation of your interests and suitability for the programme, so please answer simply and honestly in Standard English, and in your own words. Do not use any external aid, such as Artificial Intelligence or Ghostwriting; doing so may have a negative impact on your application. Each question has a character reply limit, so avoid verbose answers and write straight to the point."*

---

### Question 1: Degree Programme Preparation (Outside Curriculum)
- **Official Prompt**: *"Tell us something you have done outside your school curriculum to prepare yourself for your chosen degree programme(s)."*
- **Character Limit**: **Max 600 characters (including spaces)** (~80–100 words).
- **Core Focus**:
  - Tangible self-directed academic exploration (e.g., specific coding project, research paper, Olympiad, online specialization, self-built prototype, attachment).
  - Concrete skills acquired and direct connection to your intended major at NUS.
  - Zero fluff: Cut background storytelling; dive straight into the project, tools used, and technical insight gained.

---

### Question 2: Resilience & Learning from Failure
- **Official Prompt**: *"Describe an instance when you did not succeed in accomplishing something on your first attempt but succeeded on subsequent attempts. How and what did you learn from your initial failure, and what changes did you make to your approach to eventually succeed?"*
- **Character Limit**: **Max 600 characters (including spaces)** (~80–100 words).
- **Core Focus**:
  - The Failure: A real, specific obstacle or failed initial attempt (avoid trivial or melodramatic examples).
  - The Reflection: Honest analysis of *why* it failed (self-awareness, diagnosis).
  - The Strategic Pivot: Concrete changes made to methodology, technique, or mindset.
  - The Outcome: Measurable subsequent success and enduring lesson learned.

---

### Question 3: Meaningful Experience & Concrete Impact
- **Official Prompt**: *"Share something that is meaningful to you and explain how it has impacted you in a concrete way."*
- **Character Limit**: **Max 600 characters (including spaces)** (~80–100 words).
- **Core Focus**:
  - A genuinely significant personal pursuit, service initiative, passion, or responsibility.
  - Evidence of **concrete impact**—how it altered your actions, worldview, intellectual trajectory, or community.
  - Avoid vague moralizing; show tangible personal growth through real-world actions.

---

### Question 4: Proudest Achievement & NUS Core Values
- **Official Prompt**: *"What is your proudest achievement, and how did you accomplish it with the help or inspiration from others? Please also explain how it exemplifies some of the five NUS values of Innovation, Resilience, Excellence, Respect and Integrity."*
- **Character Limit**: **Max 1,100 characters (including spaces)** (~150–180 words).
- **Core Focus**:
  - The Achievement: Highest impact milestone (competition, initiative, artistic/scientific work, leadership).
  - Collaboration & Humility: Explicit credit given to team members, mentors, or external inspiration.
  - The **Five NUS Core Values**: Must explicitly and organically weave in relevant values:
    1. **Innovation**: Creative problem-solving, novel approaches, thinking beyond conventions.
    2. **Resilience**: Overcoming setbacks, perseverance under high pressure.
    3. **Excellence**: High standards of rigor, pursuit of mastery.
    4. **Respect**: Deep appreciation for diverse viewpoints, empathetic collaboration.
    5. **Integrity**: Ethical conduct, accountability, honesty in execution.

## 3. Key NUS Admissions Red Lines & Best Practices

| What Admissions Officers Value ✅ | What Automatically Penalizes Candidates ❌ |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Direct Answers**: First sentence directly addresses the prompt. | **Throat-clearing**: Starting with *"Ever since I was young..."* or repeating the prompt question. |
| **Standard British English**: Accurate UK spelling (*programme, analyse, specialise*). | **Americanisms**: Using *program, analyze, specialize, freshman/sophomore*. |
| **High Information Density**: Every word advances the narrative. | **Fluff & Filler**: *"In order to", "It is important to note that", "I am very passionate about"*. |
| **Concrete Metrics & Action**: *"Built a Python parser processing 10k logs"*. | **Vague Claims**: *"I did extensive research on modern technology"*. |
| **Authentic Voice**: Natural, unpretentious, human prose. | **Obvious AI Buzzwords**: *testament, delve, tapestry, pivotal, multifaceted, beacon*. |
