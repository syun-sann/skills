# IB Economics Assessment Objectives, Command Terms & Markbands

## 1. Assessment Objectives (AOs) Breakdown

| Assessment Objective | Description | Target Question Types | Cognitive Demand |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **AO1: Knowledge & Understanding** | Accurate definitions, recall of economic theories, terminology, and models. | 4m, 10m, 15m | Foundation (Must be precise, not colloquial) |
| **AO2: Application & Analysis** | Applying concepts to specific contexts, explaining causal economic transmission mechanisms step-by-step. | 4m, 10m, 15m | Explanatory (Step-by-step cause & effect) |
| **AO3: Synthesis & Evaluation** | Balanced critical thinking: weighing trade-offs, short-run vs long-run effects, stakeholder impacts, assumptions, and reaching a substantiated judgment. | **15m only** (*Forbidden in 4m and 10m*) | High-order judgment and synthesis |
| **AO4: Quantitative & Diagrammatic Skills** | Constructing, accurately labeling, and interpreting economic diagrams and calculations. | 4m, 10m, 15m | Diagram precision (Axes, curves, shifts, equilibrium) |

---

## 2. Command Terms Taxonomy

### AO1: Knowledge & Understanding
- **Define**: Give the precise meaning of a word, phrase, concept, or physical quantity.
- **Describe**: Give a detailed account.
- **Outline**: Give a brief account or summary.
- **State**: Give a specific name, value, or other brief answer without explanation or calculation.

### AO2: Application & Analysis
- **Explain**: Give a detailed account including reasons or causes. (Requires clear economic transmission chain: $A \to B \to C$).
- **Analyze**: Break down in order to bring out the essential elements or structure.
- **Distinguish**: Make clear the differences between two or more concepts or items.
- **Apply**: Use an idea, equation, principle, theory or law in relation to a given problem or issue.

### AO3: Synthesis & Evaluation
- **Evaluate**: Make an appraisal by weighing up the strengths and limitations.
- **Discuss**: Offer a considered and balanced review that includes a range of arguments, factors, or hypotheses. Opinions or conclusions should be presented clearly and supported by appropriate evidence.
- **Examine**: Consider an argument or concept in a way that uncovers the assumptions and interrelationships of the issue.
- **To what extent**: Consider the merits or otherwise of an argument or concept. Opinions and conclusions should be presented clearly and supported with empirical evidence and sound economic argument.
- **Justify**: Provide valid reasons or evidence to support an answer or conclusion.

## 3. Official IB Markband Descriptors

### 4-Mark Questions (Paper 2, Parts c–f)
Evaluated using an analytic markscheme (typically 4 marks total):
- **Diagram (2 marks)**:
  - 1 mark for correct axes labels (e.g. "Price of X ($)" and "Quantity of X") and correctly labeled original curves and equilibrium ($P_1, Q_1$).
  - 1 mark for correct shift (with direction arrow) and new equilibrium ($P_2, Q_2$).
- **Explanation (2 marks)**:
  - 1 mark for basic or partial explanation of the cause or shift.
  - 2 marks for a fully explained, step-by-step transmission mechanism linking the scenario directly to the diagram changes.
- *Strict Rule*: Zero marks awarded for evaluation. Do not write conclusions or evaluations.

---

### 10-Mark Questions (Paper 1, Part a)
Evaluated on a 0–10 holistic markband:

| Marks | Level Descriptor |
| :---: | :--- |
| **0** | The work does not reach a standard described by the descriptors below. |
| **1–2** | The response indicates little understanding of the specific demands of the question. Economic theory and terms are stated but are not relevant. |
| **3–4** | The response indicates some understanding of the specific demands of the question. Relevant economic theory is described. Some relevant economic terms are included. |
| **5–6** | The response indicates understanding of the specific demands of the question, but these demands are only partially addressed. Relevant economic theory is partly explained. Some relevant economic terms are used appropriately. Where appropriate, relevant diagram(s) are included. |
| **7–8** | The specific demands of the question are understood and addressed. Relevant economic theory is explained. Relevant economic terms are used mostly appropriately. Where appropriate, relevant diagram(s) are included and explained. |
| **9–10 (Top Band)** | **The specific demands of the question are understood and addressed. Relevant economic theory is fully explained. Relevant economic terms are used appropriately throughout the response. Where appropriate, relevant diagram(s) are included and fully explained.** |

*Golden Rule for 10-markers*: 
- High marks require: (1) Accurate introductory definitions of all command/key terms; (2) Fully labeled diagram with an explicit textual walkthrough; (3) Detailed step-by-step causal chain ($X \to Y \to Z$); (4) Concrete illustrative context or example.
- **Never include evaluation or policy pros/cons**; it wastes time and earns zero credit.

### 15-Mark Questions (Paper 1 Part b & Paper 2 Part g)
Evaluated on a 0–15 holistic markband:

| Marks | Level Descriptor |
| :---: | :--- |
| **0** | The work does not reach a standard described by the descriptors below. |
| **1–3** | Little understanding of specific demands. Economic theory and terms irrelevant. No evidence of synthesis or evaluation. Example/case data absent or irrelevant. |
| **4–6** | Some understanding of specific demands. Relevant theory described. Some relevant terms included. Superficial synthesis/evaluation. Real-world example or case data identified but superficial. |
| **7–9** | Demands partially addressed. Theory partly explained. Terms used appropriately. Diagram included but partially explained. Synthesis/evaluation present but lacks balance (one-sided). Example/case data partly developed. |
| **10–12** | Specific demands understood and addressed. Theory explained. Terms mostly appropriate. Diagram included and explained. Synthesis/evaluation is mostly balanced. Example or case data developed in the context of the question. |
| **13–15 (Top Band / Level 7)** | **The specific demands of the question are thoroughly understood and addressed. Relevant economic theory is fully explained. Relevant economic terms are used appropriately throughout the response. Where appropriate, relevant diagram(s) are included and fully explained. The response contains evidence of effective and balanced synthesis or evaluation. Relevant real-world example(s) (Paper 1) or text/data extracts (Paper 2) are identified and fully developed to support the argument.** |

*Golden Rule for 15-markers*:
- Top band requires balanced dialectical evaluation (e.g. CLASSPP: Stakeholders, Short-run vs Long-run, Assumptions, Priorities/Trade-offs).
- Arguments must be substantiated with concrete empirical facts (for Paper 1) or explicit citations of the extract text/figures (for Paper 2).
- The conclusion must make a definitive, justified economic judgment rather than just restating previous points.
