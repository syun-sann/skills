# High-Scoring IB CAS Exemplars Across Strands

This reference provides benchmark exemplar packages for each CAS strand (Creativity, Activity, Service) and a collaborative CAS Project. Each exemplar illustrates the transition from raw user notes (in Chinese or English) to a polished ManageBac **Activity Entry** and an in-depth **CAS Reflection**.

---

## Exemplar 1: Creativity (C) — Digital Art & Zine Publication

### Raw User Input (Mandarin)
> "这周我和艺术社另外两个同学负责做学校文学杂志（Literary Magazine）的排版和封面插画。之前我只习惯在iPad上手绘，但这次需要用Adobe InDesign做32页的多页排版和CMYK分色。遇到了很大困难，字体总是溢出文本框，出血位（bleed）没设好导致打印样张被切边。花了两整晚查YouTube教程重做。最终按时把第一版样刊印出来了给指导老师看。感觉学到了新排版技能，也发现自己之前太低估了出版的工程量。"

### 1. CAS Activity Entry (ManageBac Log)
* **Activity Title**: School Literary Zine Design & Pre-Press Production
* **Strand**: Creativity
* **Timeline / Duration**: Oct 12 – Oct 18, 2024 (Approx. 6 hours)
* **Role / Responsibility**: Lead Layout Designer & Illustrator
* **Summary of Actions**:
  Collaborated with two peer editors to lay out a 32-page bilingual school literary magazine in Adobe InDesign. Responsibilities included formatting submitted poetry and short fiction, setting up modular grid systems, illustrating a custom cover, and configuring CMYK print-ready PDF exports with 3mm bleed margins. Overcame initial alignment errors and text box overflow issues through iterative technical troubleshooting and test prints.
* **Direct Deliverable**: Delivered the first proof-print dummy to the faculty advisor on schedule.

### 2. CAS Reflection
> Transitioning from spontaneous digital painting on Procreate to technical editorial layout in Adobe InDesign proved far more humbling than I had anticipated. While I initially felt confident about my aesthetic sense, setting up a 32-page publication required a structural discipline I lacked: my margins were inconsistent, and failing to configure the 3mm bleed settings caused the preliminary proofs to crop my illustrations awkwardly at the spine.
>
> That moment of holding the misprinted proof at 10 PM was genuinely frustrating. It exposed a clear blind spot in my technical workflow—I had prioritized decorative visuals over mechanical typography standards (**LO 1: Identify strengths and areas for growth**). Rather than abandoning the layout or reverting to simpler templates, I spent two evenings systematically working through pre-press documentation, learning how to manage master pages, paragraph stylesheets, and CMYK color profiles (**LO 2: Undertake challenges and develop new skills**).
>
> When the revised dummy arrived from the print shop with razor-sharp margins and vibrant color fidelity, the satisfaction was immense. More importantly, this experience demystified the gap between artistic ideation and commercial production. If I were to start over, I would build a standardized typographical style sheet before placing any artwork. Going forward, I feel equipped to handle multi-page publication projects for upcoming campus advocacy campaigns.

* **Recommended Portfolio Evidence**:
  - Side-by-side photo comparison of the misprinted proof vs. the final corrected dummy.
  - Screenshots of the InDesign master page grid layout and paragraph styles panel.
  - Faculty advisor confirmation email.

## Exemplar 2: Activity (A) — Half-Marathon Training & Aerobic Conditioning

### Raw User Input (English)
> "Did my week 8 long run on Saturday. Target was 16km at 5:30/km pace. Weather was humid and 28C. Around km 11 my right hamstring tightened up badly and I wanted to stop. Slowed down to 6:15 pace, focused on cadence and hydration at the water fountain, and managed to finish 16.2km in 1h32m. Realized I hadn't been doing enough dynamic stretching and foam rolling during the week."

### 1. CAS Activity Entry (ManageBac Log)
* **Activity Title**: Long-Distance Endurance Conditioning: 16km Progressive Run
* **Strand**: Activity
* **Timeline / Duration**: Nov 4, 2024 (1.5 hours)
* **Role / Responsibility**: Individual Athlete / Self-Directed Training
* **Summary of Actions**:
  Completed a 16.2 km outdoor endurance session as part of week 8 of my half-marathon preparatory cycle. Monitored heart rate zones and split pacing in high heat (28°C, 82% humidity). When acute hamstring tightness occurred at the 11 km mark, adjusted pacing strategy dynamically, regulated cadence to 175 spm, and executed targeted hydration to prevent cramping.
* **Direct Deliverable**: Completed 16.2 km in 1:32:18; logged cardiovascular data and recovery metrics.

### 2. CAS Reflection
> Saturday's 16 km long run served as a stark reminder that endurance training is governed as much by disciplined recovery as by sheer mileage. The morning was unseasonably humid (28°C), and by kilometer 11, a sharp pull in my right hamstring threatened to derail the session. My immediate impulse was frustration—I was tracking behind my target 5:30/km split and feared losing my weekly progression.
>
> Instead of stubbornly forcing the pace and risking a tear, I made the conscious decision to recalibrate (**LO 1: Identify own strengths and develop areas for growth**). I decelerated to 6:15/km, stopped at the public fountain to rehydrate, and focused entirely on quickening my foot strike cadence to reduce ground contact shock. Pushing through the final 5 km under discomfort required sustained mental discipline (**LO 4: Show commitment and perseverance**).
>
> Reviewing my training log afterward made the root cause obvious: during a hectic week of school assignments, I had repeatedly skipped my post-run foam rolling and dynamic mobility routines. I realized that athletic progress is an interdependent ecosystem—skipping recovery sabotages performance. I have now scheduled mandatory 15-minute mobility blocks into my morning calendar before each training day.

* **Recommended Portfolio Evidence**:
  - GPS watch tracking screenshot showing split pace, elevation, and heart rate graph.
  - Photo of weekly training journal entry with annotations on recovery adjustments.

## Exemplar 3: Service (S) — Bilingual Community Science Workshops

### Raw User Input (Mixed Chinese/English)
> "今天下午去社区中心给打工子弟小学的三年级小朋友上趣味物理课（做水火箭和气球动力小车）。我是组长，和另外三个人一起去。一开始讲牛顿第三定律小朋友根本听不懂，跑来跑去很乱。后来我们临时决定不讲公式，改成分小组比赛谁的气球车跑得最远，让他们在动手组装的过程中自己发现‘反作用力’。有个叫小明的男生本来很害羞不说话，后来他那组车跑了第一名，他开心地跟我们讲了好多。反思点：教小孩子不能照本宣科，要用他们听得懂的语言；另外做义工不是去‘施舍’知识，而是建立平等的信任。"

### 1. CAS Activity Entry (ManageBac Log)
* **Activity Title**: Hands-On STEM Outreach: Newton’s Third Law Workshop
* **Strand**: Service
* **Timeline / Duration**: Dec 1, 2024 (3 hours on-site)
* **Role / Responsibility**: Workshop Leader & Lesson Facilitator
* **Summary of Actions**:
  Led a team of four student volunteers in delivering a hands-on physics workshop for twenty 3rd-grade students at the Dongcheng Community Center. When initial theoretical explanations failed to engage the children, facilitated an immediate pedagogical pivot: transitioned from formal lectures to an inquiry-based balloon car building challenge. Guided small groups through material assembly, troubleshooting air leaks, and testing propulsion dynamics.
* **Direct Deliverable**: 20 students successfully constructed working balloon cars and demonstrated basic intuitive comprehension of action-reaction forces.

### 2. CAS Reflection
> Standing in front of twenty energetic eight-year-olds at the Dongcheng Community Center, our carefully structured PowerPoint on Newton’s Third Law collapsed within ten minutes. Seeing blank stares and restless fidgeting, I felt a wave of inadequacy—we had prepared like IB physics students rather than empathetic communicators.
>
> Recognizing that our rigid curriculum was failing the children, our team gathered for a hasty two-minute consultation and pivoted (**LO 5: Demonstrate the skills and benefits of working collaboratively**). We put away the slides and distributed cardboard wheels, straws, and balloons, reframing the session as a "Rocket Racer Derby." By letting the kids physically feel the air rushing backward as the cars shot forward, abstract theory transformed into tangible wonder. Watching Xiaoming—who had spent the first hour quietly staring at his shoes—beam with pride as his customized chassis crossed the finish line first was a deeply rewarding moment.
>
> This experience fundamentally reshaped my understanding of service ethics (**LO 7: Recognize and consider the ethics of choices and actions**). Meaningful community engagement is never a patronizing transmission of superior knowledge; it requires dismantling one's own assumptions, listening to the audience's emotional wavelength, and meeting learners where they are. In future workshops, our team will structure all lesson plans around inquiry-first experiments rather than didactic lecturing.

* **Recommended Portfolio Evidence**:
  - Anonymized photographs of student-built balloon cars and testing track.
  - Revised hands-on lesson plan worksheet showing the "before and after" adaptation.
  - Community center supervisor verification letter.

## Exemplar 4: CAS Project Milestone — Investigation & Planning Stage

### Raw User Input (Mandarin)
> "我们的CAS Project是关于校园厨余垃圾减量和堆肥的（Campus Composting & Food Waste Initiative），为期两个月。这周我们在做第一阶段（Investigation & Preparation）。我和4个组员在食堂连续5天称重午餐倒掉的剩饭剩菜，发现平均每天浪费多达48公斤！我们还调查了学校物业和后勤，发现他们目前全部当做普通垃圾运往填埋场。我们写了第一份《校园堆肥可行性建议书》，列了堆肥箱预算、选址在生物园角落、防异味和安全方案，下周准备约总务处主任开会。"

### 1. CAS Activity Entry (ManageBac Log)
* **Activity Title**: CAS Project Stage 1: Campus Food Waste Audit & Feasibility Study
* **Strand**: Service & Creativity (Collaborative CAS Project)
* **Timeline / Duration**: Jan 8 – Jan 19, 2025 (Stage 1: Investigation & Planning)
* **Role / Responsibility**: Project Co-Initiator & Lead Data Auditor
* **Summary of Actions**:
  Co-initiated a multi-month environmental project targeting school cafeteria food waste. Over five consecutive days, organized daily waste-sorting audits at lunch dismissal, measuring and cataloging an average of 48.2 kg of daily organic waste. Researched aerobic composting methodologies, conducted campus site assessments, drafted a risk mitigation protocol for pest control and odor management, and synthesized findings into a formal 6-page proposal for school administration.
* **Direct Deliverable**: Completed 5-day empirical waste audit dataset and submitted the Composting Feasibility Proposal to the Facilities Department.

### 2. CAS Reflection
> Launching the investigation phase of our CAS Project brought into sharp focus the reality of environmental waste on our own campus. Scraping uneaten trays and recording a staggering daily average of 48.2 kg of discarded food was visceral and unsettling. It bridged the gap between abstract discussions in Environmental Systems & Societies (ESS) and our collective daily footprint, connecting directly to UN Sustainable Development Goal 12 (**LO 6: Demonstrate engagement with issues of global significance**).
>
> The greatest challenge during this stage was moving from passionate idealism to rigorous administrative planning (**LO 3: Demonstrate how to initiate and plan a CAS experience**). When we initially pitched the idea to the facilities manager, he raised legitimate concerns about rodent infestation, odor complaints, and custodial workload. Rather than viewing this as rejection, our group treated it as essential feedback. We spent the weekend researching dual-chamber rotating compost tumblers and formulating a strict volunteer rotation schedule to guarantee daily maintenance without burdening school staff (**LO 5: Demonstrate collaborative problem-solving**).
>
> This investigative phase taught me that initiating sustainable change requires empathy for administrative stakeholders as much as ecological passion. Having secured our preliminary review meeting for next Tuesday, our team is prepared to present data-driven solutions rather than vague intentions.

* **Recommended Portfolio Evidence**:
  - 5-day cafeteria waste audit spreadsheet and data charts.
  - The submitted 6-page Feasibility Proposal document (PDF).
  - Meeting confirmation and email correspondence with the School Facilities Director.
